How PhishGuard works

1

Paste

Drop in the sender, subject, and body of a suspicious email. Nothing is uploaded or stored; the text is analyzed in memory only.

2

Analyze

An interpretable machine-learning model reads the wording and structure and sorts the email into Safe, Scam, or Malware with a confidence score.

3

Understand and act

You get plain-language red flags explaining the decision, transparent sender checks, and a clear recommended action.

What the verdicts mean

Safe

No strong phishing or malware signals were found in the text. Ordinary correspondence still deserves care with unexpected requests.

Scam

Social-engineering signals such as urgency, credential or payment requests, and lookalike senders, typical of phishing and fraud.

Malware

Wording typical of malware-delivery lures, for example attachment and macro prompts. This is a small-data proof of concept based on wording, not attachment scanning.

Spot phishing yourself

  • • Check the real sender address, not just the display name.
  • • Be wary of urgency, threats, or "act now" pressure.
  • • Hover over links to see where they really go.
  • • Never enable macros or open unexpected attachments.
  • • Watch for lookalike domains (paypa1 vs paypal).
  • • Verify money or password requests through a trusted channel.
